Q1 (Unit 1): Explain the concept, sources, and epistemology (Pramanas) of the
Indian Knowledge System, along with its philosophical foundations.
Introduction
The Indian Knowledge System (IKS) represents a holistic, continuous tradition of inquiry uniting
material progress (Abhyudaya) with spiritual liberation (Moksha). Unlike purely empirical
modern silos, IKS integrates philosophy, science, ethics, and lived practice.
Key Sources of IKS
Shruti (Revealed Literature): The four Vedas (Rig, Yajur, Sama, Atharva), Brahmanas,
Aranyakas, and Upanishads.
Smriti & Shastras: Vedangas, Epics (Ramayana, Mahabharata), Puranas, and specialized
treatises like Arthashastra and Charaka Samhita.
Heterodox & Oral Traditions: Buddhist Tripitaka, Jain Agamas, and regional folk/tribal oral
knowledge systems.
Epistemology (Pramana Shastra) & Logic
Indian epistemology defines six valid means of knowledge (Pramanas): Pratyaksha (direct
perception), Anumana (inference), Shabda (verbal testimony), Upamana (analogy), Arthapatti
(postulation), and Anupalabdhi (non-perception). Logic (Tarka) and Ontology (Dravyavijnana)
ensure error-free reasoning.
Philosophical Foundations
Samkhya explains reality through Purusha (consciousness) and Prakriti (matter with three
Gunas). Yoga provides the eight-limbed discipline to still the mind, leading to Moksha—
ultimate freedom from suffering.
Conclusion
With its rigorous epistemology and holistic worldview, IKS remains highly relevant today for
preventive health, ethics, and multidisciplinary education.

Q2 (Unit 2): Discuss India's ancient contributions to Mathematics, Astronomy,
Metallurgy, Ayurveda, and Maritime Trade.
Introduction
Ancient India achieved remarkable heights in exact sciences, medicine, and oceanic
commerce, integrating theoretical depth with practical utility.
Mathematics & Astronomy
Mathematics: Invention of zero (Shunya) and the decimal system; Sulba Sutras
(geometry), Aryabhata (trigonometry, pi), Brahmagupta (negative numbers), and
Bhaskaracharya (Lilavati).
Astronomy: Texts like Surya Siddhanta and Aryabhatiya accurately modeled planetary
motion, eclipses, and solar-lunar calendars (Panchanga).
Metallurgy & Ayurveda
Indian metallurgists produced rust-resistant iron (Delhi Iron Pillar), high-carbon Wootz steel,
and pioneered zinc distillation at Zawar. In health sciences, Ayurveda (Charaka and Sushruta
Samhitas) established holistic medicine and surgical techniques based on balancing the
Tridoshas (Vata, Pitta, Kapha).
Maritime Trade & Architecture
From Lothal's dockyard to ports like Tamralipti and Palur, Indian guilds (Shreni) traded across
the Silk and Spice Routes, guided by shipbuilding texts like Yukti Kalpataru. This prosperity
fostered Nagara, Dravida, and Vesara temple architecture.
Conclusion
India's scientific and maritime heritage demonstrates a sophisticated synthesis of empirical
observation, craftsmanship, and global cultural exchange.
Q3 (Unit 3): Analyze the concepts of Dharma, Rajadharma, and environmental
sustainability in ancient Indian socio-political and agricultural thought.
Introduction
Ancient Indian polity and ecology were anchored in Dharma—the principle of cosmic, moral,
and social order that bound both ruler and citizen.
Rajadharma & Statecraft
Ethical Kingship: Rajadharma required the ruler to prioritize public welfare (Praja-hita)
above personal interest.
Saptanga Theory: Kautilya's Arthashastra defined seven pillars of the state (Swamin,
Amatya, Janapada, Durga, Kosha, Danda, Mitra) alongside pragmatic diplomacy and justice

Social Order & Indigenous Agriculture
Society functioned through mutual duties across the four Ashramas and Purusharthas. In
agriculture, texts like Krishi-Parashara and Vrikshayurveda promoted monsoon forecasting,
seed conservation, crop rotation, and organic fertilizers (Kunapajala).
Environmental Ethics & Water Management
Reverence for the five elements (Panchamahabhuta) and the Vedic Prithvi Sukta fostered
ecological stewardship. Traditional water systems—stepwells (Baolis), tanks, Ahar-Pyne, and

Conclusion
Combining ethical governance with eco-friendly farming and water conservation, ancient
Indian thought offers a timeless blueprint for sustainable development.
Q4 (Unit 4): Examine the role of Panini's Grammar, Nyaya Logic, Classical
Music, and Yoga in shaping India's intellectual and cultural traditions.
Introduction
India's intellectual and artistic traditions share a commitment to precision, structured
reasoning, and inner harmony.
Language & Logic
Paninian Grammar: Panini's Ashtadhyayi (~4,000 sutras) formalized Sanskrit as a
generative, rule-based system, complemented by Patanjali's Mahabhashya and Bhartrihari's
Sphota philosophy of meaning.
Nyaya Logic: Gautama's Nyaya Sutras and later Navya-Nyaya established a five-part
syllogism and structured rules of debate for arriving at truth.
Music, Dance & Aesthetics
Rooted in the Samaveda and Bharata's Natyashastra, Indian classical music uses Swara, Raga,
and Tala, while classical dance evokes the nine aesthetic sentiments (Navarasa) through
Abhinaya and mudras.
Yoga & Indigenous Sports
Patanjali's Ashtanga Yoga unites body, breath, and mind, while indigenous games like
Chaturanga (chess), Mallakhamb, and Kabaddi cultivate physical agility and strategic focus.
Conclusion
Today, Paninian rules inspire computational linguistics (NLP), while Yoga and Indian classical
arts enrich global wellness and culture.

Q5 (Unit 5): Describe the Odisha Knowledge System, highlighting Odisha's
scholarly contributions, maritime trade (Sadhabas), and rural governance.
Introduction
The Odisha Knowledge System reflects centuries of indigenous innovation across ancient
Kalinga, Utkala, and Kosala, blending Sanskritic learning with vibrant regional traditions.
Scholarly & Historical Contributions
Astronomy & Literature: Pathani Samanta (Samanta Chandrasekhar) authored Siddhanta
Darpana using simple bamboo instruments. Literary icons like Jayadeva (Gita Govinda),
Sarala Das, and the Panchasakha democratized knowledge in Odia.
Learning Centers: Buddhist universities at Ratnagiri, Lalitgiri, and Udayagiri and Emperor
Kharavela's patronage shaped a pluralistic intellectual heritage.
Maritime Heritage & Governance
Odisha's Sadhabas sailed in Boitas from ports like Tamralipti, Palur, and Manikapatna to Bali,
Java, and Sumatra—celebrated today during Boita Bandana and Baliyatra. Governance
combined Rajadharma with grassroots institutions like Bhagabata Tungis and village councils.
Conclusion
Odisha's knowledge system stands out for its seamless integration of scientific precision,
oceanic enterprise, and community-led rural wisdom.
Q6 (Unit 6): Discuss Kalinga Temple Architecture, Odissi performing arts, folk
traditions, and Jagannath Culture as an integrated knowledge system.
Introduction
Odisha's artistic and cultural heritage exemplifies a living synthesis of engineering, aesthetics,
devotion, and community life.
Kalinga Architecture & Odissi Arts
Kalinga Temple Style: Characterized by Rekha Deula (Lingaraj, Puri), Pidha Deula (Konark
Jagamohana), and Khakhara Deula (Vaital Deula), alongside early rock-cut caves at
Udayagiri-Khandagiri.
Odissi Dance & Music: Evolved from Mahari and Gotipua traditions, Odissi is famed for
Tribhanga and Chowka postures accompanied by classical Odissi ragas and Mardala.
Folk & Material Traditions
Odisha's folk culture thrives through Pattachitra, Pipili Applique, Tarakasi filigree, Dhokra
casting, and performing arts like Mayurbhanj Chhau, Pala, Daskathia, and Sambalpuri songs
(Dalkhai).

Jagannath Culture
Lord Jagannath's tradition integrates tribal, Vedic, and Vaishnavite elements—seen in
Mahaprasad (56 offerings shared without caste barriers in Ananda Bazar), Parshva Devatas,
and Dwadasa Yatra (including Ratha Yatra).
Conclusion
Together, these traditions preserve Odisha's living spiritual, artistic, and egalitarian ethos
across generations.
Q7 (Unit 7): Explain how Odisha's tribal wisdom, agricultural cycle, water
systems, festivals (Nuakhai, Ratha Yatra), and textiles reflect socio-ecological
knowledge.
Introduction
In Odisha, ecology, livelihood, and culture are deeply intertwined, creating resilient community-
based knowledge systems.
Tribal Ethics & Water Management
Tribal Knowledge: Odisha's 62 tribal communities protect biodiversity through sacred
groves (Jahera), ethnomedicine, and sustainable forest use.
Water Harvesting: Traditional structures like Kata, Munda, Bandha, and Chahala capture
monsoon water for collective irrigation.
Agricultural Cycle, Festivals & Food Culture
Farming follows seasonal rituals—from sowing on Akshaya Tritiya to celebrating the first
harvest at Nuakhai. Festivals like Ratha Yatra promote social unity, while seasonal foods
(Pakhala, Dalma, Pithas) align nutrition with climate.
Handicrafts & Textile Heritage
Handlooms like Sambalpuri Ikat (Bandha), Bomkai, Kotpad (natural madder dye), and
Maniabandha showcase geometric precision and eco-friendly artisan livelihoods.
Conclusion
Odisha's socio-cultural traditions offer practical models for climate resilience, sustainable
agriculture, and community solidarity.

Q8 (Unit 8): Evaluate the integration of Artificial Intelligence (AI) with the
Indian Knowledge System, including domain applications and ethical
guardrails.
Introduction
Integrating Artificial Intelligence (AI) with the Indian Knowledge System (IKS) enables the
preservation, analysis, and modern application of centuries-old wisdom.
Key Domain Applications
Health & Science: AI-driven Ayurgenomics links Ayurvedic Prakriti types with genetic data;
ML accelerates herbal drug discovery.
Agriculture & Heritage: Combining traditional monsoon/crop calendars with satellite data
for precision farming, and 3D AI reconstruction of damaged temples and ancient maritime
routes.
Language, Arts & Odisha IKS: Paninian grammar inspires NLP; computer vision aids Yoga
and Odissi mudra training; OCR and translation digitize Odia palm-leaf manuscripts (Pothi)
and endangered tribal languages.
Ethical & Cultural Guardrails
Responsible AI in IKS requires protecting cultural authenticity (avoiding distortion of sacred
texts), ensuring data sovereignty and IP rights for indigenous communities against biopiracy,
and mitigating linguistic bias.
Conclusion
When guided by strong ethical frameworks, AI can safeguard India's and Odisha's diverse
knowledge heritage for future generations.
