UNIT I
 Philosophical Understanding of Research

1. Philosophical Concepts of Research

 English Research philosophy refers to the system of beliefs and assumptions about the
 development of knowledge and the nature of reality. Before choosing how to collect data (surveys
 vs. interviews), a researcher must understand what counts as valid truth and reality. The three
 core pillars of research philosophy are Ontology (what is reality?), Epistemology (how do we
 know reality?), and Axiology (what role do values and ethics play?).

 Hinglish (Teacher Style) Dekho students, jab bhi hum koi research shuru karte hain, toh seedha
 questionnaire ya survey banane nahi bhaagte. Sabse pehle humein apna nazarariya
 (worldview/philosophy) clear karna padta hai. Research Philosophy ka simple matlab hai—aap
 duniya ki sachai (reality) ko kaise dekhte ho aur aapke hisaab se "sahi gyaan (valid knowledge)"
 kya hai? Jaise ek building ki neev (foundation) hoti hai, waise hi philosophy aapki research ki neev
 hai.

 Example Suppose two researchers study poverty in rural Odisha. Researcher A believes
 poverty is strictly a number (income below ₹X per day)—this is a Positivist philosophy. Researcher
 B believes poverty is a lived emotional experience of dignity and struggle—this is an Interpretivist
 philosophy.

2. Ontology (Nature of Reality)

 English Ontology is the branch of philosophy concerned with the nature of reality and
 existence ("What exists?" or "What is the nature of social reality?"). In research, ontology asks
 whether social reality is objective (existing independently of human perception, known as
 Objectivism/Realism) or subjective (socially constructed by human minds and interactions, known
 as Constructivism/Subjectivism).

 Hinglish (Teacher Style) Ontology ko yaad rakhne ka sabse aasan tarika hai ek sawaal: "Asliyat
 (Reality) kya hai?" Kya sachai humare dimaag se bahar ek thos cheez hai jo sabke liye same hai
 (Objectivism), ya phir sachai logon ki soch aur samaj ke banayein niyamo se banti hai
 (Constructivism)? Agar aap maante ho ki sachai ek hi hai chahe koi dekhe ya na dekhe, toh aap
 Objectivist ontology follow kar rahe ho.

 Example

 Objective Ontology: Gravity exists, or the population of a village is 1,250 people—this is an
 external fact independent of our feelings.

 Subjective (Constructivist) Ontology: "Workplace Happiness" or "Gender Roles" do not exist as
 physical objects; they are created by people's perceptions, culture, and daily interactions.

3. Epistemology (Theory of Knowledge)

 English Epistemology is the study of the nature, sources, and validity of knowledge ("How do
 we know what we know?" and "What constitutes acceptable knowledge?"). It examines the
 relationship between the researcher and what is being researched:

Positivism: Knowledge comes only from observable, measurable, scientific facts (uses
 quantitative data).

 Interpretivism: Knowledge comes from understanding human meanings, motives, and social
 contexts (uses qualitative data).

 Hinglish (Teacher Style) Ontology ne pucha "Reality kya hai?", ab Epistemology puchti hai "Us
 reality ke baare mein hum Sahi Knowledge kaise haasil karein?" Matlab gyaan paane ka rasta kya
 hai? Agar aap kehte ho ki sirf data, numbers aur lab experiments se hi sachai pata chalegi, toh yeh
 Positivist Epistemology hai. Aur agar aap kehte ho ki insaano se baat karke, unke dukh-sukh
 samajh ke hi sachai pata chalegi, toh yeh Interpretivist Epistemology hai.

 Example To study student exam stress:

 Positivist Epistemology: Measuring blood pressure, cortisol levels, and test scores of 500
 students.

 Interpretivist Epistemology: Sitting down with 15 students for deep interviews to understand why
 family expectations make them anxious.

4. Hermeneutics Theory

 English Hermeneutics is the theory and methodology of interpretation, originally developed for
 interpreting religious and historical texts, and later expanded by philosophers like Hans-Georg
 Gadamer and Paul Ricoeur to all human actions, texts, and cultural artifacts. A core concept is the
 Hermeneutic Circle, which states that we can only understand the whole text/event by
 understanding its individual parts, and we can only understand the parts by placing them in the
 context of the whole (historical, cultural, and authorial context).

 Hinglish (Teacher Style) Beta, Hermeneutics ka seedha matlab hota hai "Art of Interpretation"
 (kisi text, bhashan, ya insaani vyavahar ka gehra matlab nikalna). Maan lo aapko 200 saal purani
 koi diary ya tribal folk song mila. Agar aap sirf dictionary se word-by-word translate karoge toh asli
 matlab kabhi samajh nahi aayega! Aapko us waqt ke itihaas, culture, aur likhne wale ke haalaat ko
 samajhna padega. Isko Hermeneutic Circle kehte hain—poore context se ek line ko samajhna, aur
 har line se poore context ko samajhna.

 Example A researcher studying Mahatma Gandhi's letters from prison cannot just read the
 literal English words; using Hermeneutics, the researcher interprets each sentence in the context
 of British colonial rule, the freedom struggle, and Gandhi's personal spiritual philosophy.
