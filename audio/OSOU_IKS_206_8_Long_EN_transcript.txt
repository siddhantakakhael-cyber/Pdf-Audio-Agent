Question one, from Unit One. Explain the concept, sources, and epistemology of the Indian Knowledge System, along with its philosophical foundations. Introduction. The Indian Knowledge System, or I K S, is a holistic and continuous tradition of inquiry that unites material progress, called Abhyudaya, with spiritual liberation, called Moksha. Unlike modern subjects that study things in separate boxes, I K S brings together philosophy, science, ethics and lived practice into one framework. Key sources of I K S. First, Shruti, the revealed literature. This includes the four Vedas, namely Rig, Yajur, Sama and Atharva, along with the Brahmanas, the Aranyakas and the Upanishads. Second, Smriti and the Shastras. This includes the Vedangas, the epics Ramayana and Mahabharata, the Puranas, and specialised treatises such as the Arthashastra and the Charaka Samhita. Third, heterodox and oral traditions. This includes the Buddhist Tripitaka, the Jain Agamas, and the regional folk and tribal knowledge that flows from generation to generation by word of mouth. Epistemology and logic. Indian epistemology describes six valid means of knowledge, called Pramanas. Pratyaksha means direct perception. Anumana means inference. Shabda means verbal testimony. Upamana means analogy. Arthapatti means postulation. And Anupalabdhi means non-perception. Along with these, logic, called Tarka, and ontology keep our reasoning free from error. Philosophical foundations. The Samkhya school explains reality through Purusha, which is pure consciousness, and Prakriti, which is matter with its three qualities, the Gunas. Yoga gives us an eight-limbed discipline that stills the mind and leads to Moksha, the ultimate freedom from suffering. Conclusion. With its rigorous way of testing knowledge and its holistic view of life, I K S remains highly relevant today in preventive health, in ethics, and in multidisciplinary education.

Question two, from Unit Two. Discuss India's ancient contributions to mathematics, astronomy, metallurgy, Ayurveda and maritime trade. Introduction. Ancient India reached remarkable heights in the exact sciences, in medicine and in ocean trade, always joining deep theory with practical use. Mathematics and astronomy. In mathematics, India gave the world zero, called Shunya, and the decimal system. The Sulba Sutras developed geometry. Aryabhata worked on trigonometry and the value of pi. Brahmagupta studied negative numbers. And Bhaskaracharya wrote the famous Lilavati. In astronomy, texts such as the Surya Siddhanta and the Aryabhatiya modelled planetary motion, eclipses and the solar and lunar calendar, the Panchanga, with great accuracy. Metallurgy and Ayurveda. Indian metallurgists produced rust-resistant iron, seen today in the Delhi Iron Pillar, and high-carbon Wootz steel, and they pioneered zinc distillation at Zawar. In health sciences, Ayurveda, through the Charaka and Sushruta Samhitas, established holistic medicine and surgical techniques based on balancing the three Doshas, namely Vata, Pitta and Kapha. Maritime trade and architecture. From the dockyard at Lothal to ports like Tamralipti and Palur, Indian guilds, the Shrenis, traded across the Silk Route and the Spice Route, guided by shipbuilding texts such as the Yukti Kalpataru. This prosperity gave birth to the Nagara, Dravida and Vesara styles of temple architecture. Conclusion. India's scientific and maritime heritage shows a fine synthesis of close observation, skilled craftsmanship and global cultural exchange.

Question three, from Unit Three. Analyse the concepts of Dharma, Rajadharma and environmental sustainability in ancient Indian social, political and agricultural thought. Introduction. Ancient Indian polity and ecology were anchored in Dharma, the principle of cosmic, moral and social order, which bound both the ruler and the citizen. Rajadharma and statecraft. Ethical kingship meant that Rajadharma required a ruler to keep public welfare, called Praja hita, above personal interest. The Saptanga theory in Kautilya's Arthashastra described seven pillars of the state, namely Swamin the ruler, Amatya the minister, Janapada the territory, Durga the fort, Kosha the treasury, Danda the army or justice, and Mitra the ally. Along with the Dharmashastra, this defined the limits of justice and ethical rule. Social order and indigenous agriculture. Society ran on mutual duties across the four Ashramas, the stages of life, and the four Purusharthas, the goals of life. In agriculture, texts such as Krishi Parashara and Vrikshayurveda promoted monsoon forecasting, seed conservation, crop rotation and organic fertilisers, including a preparation called Kunapajala. Environmental ethics and water management. Reverence for the five great elements, the Panchamahabhuta, and the Vedic Prithvi Sukta, encouraged the careful stewardship of nature. Traditional water systems, such as stepwells called Baolis, tanks, the Ahar-Pyne system of channels, and sacred groves, ensured long-term sustainability. Conclusion. By combining ethical governance with eco-friendly farming and water conservation, ancient Indian thought offers a timeless blueprint for sustainable development.

Question four, from Unit Four. Examine the role of Panini's grammar, Nyaya logic, classical music and Yoga in shaping India's intellectual and cultural traditions. Introduction. India's intellectual and artistic traditions share a commitment to precision, structured reasoning and inner harmony. Language and logic. Panini's grammar, the Ashtadhyayi, contains about four thousand sutras, and it turned Sanskrit into a formal, generative, rule-based system. Patanjali's Mahabhashya and Bhartrihari's Sphota philosophy of meaning enriched this study of language. Nyaya logic. Gautama's Nyaya Sutras and the later Navya Nyaya school established a five-part syllogism and structured rules of debate for arriving at truth. Music, dance and aesthetics. Rooted in the Samaveda and in Bharata's Natyashastra, Indian classical music is built on Swara, Raga and Tala, while classical dance evokes the nine aesthetic sentiments, the Navarasa, through Abhinaya, expressive acting, and mudras, hand gestures. Yoga and indigenous sports. Patanjali's Ashtanga Yoga unites body, breath and mind. Indigenous games such as Chaturanga, which is chess, Mallakhamb, Kabaddi and Kalaripayattu build physical agility and strategic focus. Conclusion. Today, Panini's rules inspire computational linguistics and natural language processing, while Yoga and the Indian classical arts enrich global wellness and culture.

Question five, from Unit Five. Describe the Odisha Knowledge System, highlighting Odisha's scholarly contributions, maritime trade of the Sadhabas, and rural governance. Introduction. The Odisha Knowledge System reflects centuries of indigenous innovation across ancient Kalinga, Utkala and Kosala, blending classical Sanskrit learning with vibrant regional traditions. Scholarly and historical contributions. In astronomy and literature, Pathani Samanta, also known as Samanta Chandrasekhar, wrote the Siddhanta Darpana using simple instruments made of bamboo. Literary figures such as Jayadeva, who wrote the Gita Govinda, Sarala Das, who wrote the Odia Mahabharata, and the Panchasakha poets carried knowledge to ordinary people in their own language. As learning centres, the Buddhist universities at Ratnagiri, Lalitgiri and Udayagiri, and the patronage of Emperor Kharavela, shaped a pluralistic intellectual heritage. Maritime heritage and governance. Odisha's Sadhaba merchants sailed in Boitas, large boats, from ports such as Tamralipti, Palur and Manikapatna to Bali, Java and Sumatra. That memory is still celebrated in Boita Bandana and the Baliyatra festival. Governance combined Rajadharma with grassroots institutions such as the Bhagabata Tungis, village meeting houses, and village councils. Conclusion. Odisha's knowledge system stands out for its seamless integration of scientific precision, oceanic enterprise and community-led rural wisdom.

Question six, from Unit Six. Discuss Kalinga temple architecture, Odissi performing arts, folk traditions and Jagannath culture as an integrated knowledge system. Introduction. Odisha's artistic and cultural heritage is a living synthesis of engineering, aesthetics, devotion and community life. Kalinga architecture and Odissi arts. The Kalinga temple style has three main forms. The Rekha Deula is the tall curvilinear tower, seen at Lingaraj and at the Jagannath temple in Puri. The Pidha Deula is the stepped pyramidal roof, seen in the Jagamohana hall at Konark. And the Khakhara Deula is the barrel-shaped shrine, seen at Vaital Deula. Alongside these are the early rock-cut caves at Udayagiri and Khandagiri. Odissi dance and music evolved from the Mahari and Gotipua traditions, and Odissi is famous for its Tribhanga and Chowka postures, accompanied by classical Odissi ragas and the Mardala drum. Folk and material traditions. Odisha's folk culture thrives through Pattachitra painting, Pipili applique work, Tarakasi silver filigree, Dhokra metal casting, and performing arts such as Mayurbhanj Chhau, Pala, Daskathia and the Sambalpuri Dalkhai songs. Jagannath culture. The tradition of Lord Jagannath integrates tribal, Vedic and Vaishnavite elements. We see this in the Mahaprasad, fifty-six offerings shared without any caste barrier in the Ananda Bazar, in the Parshva Devatas, the attendant deities, and in the twelve festivals of the Dwadasa Yatra, including the Ratha Yatra. Conclusion. Together, these traditions preserve Odisha's living spiritual, artistic and egalitarian ethos across generations.

Question seven, from Unit Seven. Explain how Odisha's tribal wisdom, agricultural cycle, water systems, festivals such as Nuakhai and Ratha Yatra, and textiles reflect socio-ecological knowledge. Introduction. In Odisha, ecology, livelihood and culture are deeply intertwined, creating resilient knowledge systems that are owned by the community. Tribal ethics and water management. Odisha's sixty-two tribal communities protect biodiversity through sacred groves called Jahera, through ethnomedicine, and through the sustainable use of forests. For water harvesting, traditional structures such as Kata, Munda, Bandha and Chahala capture monsoon water for collective irrigation. Agricultural cycle, festivals and food culture. Farming follows seasonal rituals, from sowing on Akshaya Tritiya to celebrating the first harvest at Nuakhai. Festivals such as Ratha Yatra promote social unity, while seasonal foods such as Pakhala, Dalma and Pitha match nutrition to the climate. Handicrafts and textile heritage. Handlooms such as Sambalpuri Ikat, also called Bandha, Bomkai, Kotpad with its natural madder dye, and Maniabandha, all showcase geometric precision and eco-friendly artisan livelihoods. Conclusion. Odisha's socio-cultural traditions offer practical models for climate resilience, sustainable agriculture and community solidarity.

Question eight, from Unit Eight. Evaluate the integration of Artificial Intelligence with the Indian Knowledge System, including domain applications and ethical guardrails. Introduction. Integrating Artificial Intelligence with the Indian Knowledge System allows centuries-old wisdom to be preserved, analysed and used in modern ways. Key domain applications. In health and science, AI-driven Ayurgenomics links Ayurvedic Prakriti types with genetic data, and machine learning speeds up herbal drug discovery. In agriculture and heritage, traditional monsoon and crop calendars are combined with satellite data for precision farming, and AI helps in the three-dimensional reconstruction of damaged temples and ancient maritime routes. In language, arts and Odisha's knowledge system, Panini's grammar inspires natural language processing, computer vision helps train Yoga and Odissi mudras, and O C R with translation digitises Odia palm-leaf manuscripts, the Pothi, along with endangered tribal languages. Ethical and cultural guardrails. Responsible AI in I K S requires protecting cultural authenticity and avoiding distortion of sacred texts. It requires ensuring data sovereignty and intellectual property rights for indigenous communities, so that biopiracy is prevented. And it requires reducing linguistic bias. Conclusion. When guided by strong ethical frameworks, AI can safeguard India's and Odisha's diverse knowledge heritage for future generations.
